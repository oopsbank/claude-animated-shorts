There's a town in Norway where you're not allowed to die. And honestly? They have a very good reason.

This is Longyearbyen. One of the northernmost towns on Earth. Where people carry rifles, in case of polar bears.

Here's the crazy part. The ground here never thaws. It's frozen solid, all year round.

In 1918, the Spanish flu reached the town. Young miners died, and were buried in the little cemetery.

But decades later, people noticed something creepy. The bodies weren't rotting. The ground was keeping them, like a giant freezer.

And a freezer full of flu victims is not a great neighbor.

So in 1950, the town stopped burying people. In 1998, scientists even flew in and dug up those miners, hunting for the virus that killed fifty million people.

Technically, dying isn't against the law. But if you get very old, or very sick, you get a plane ticket to the mainland.

And it works both ways. Pregnant women fly south weeks before they give birth.

So almost nobody is born here. Almost nobody dies here. Which is why there's a town in Norway where you're not allowed to die.
