The most dangerous drink in this bar isn't whiskey. It's water. Your body is mostly water, and it guards one number like treasure: how salty that water is. Drink a glass. Your kidneys handle it. A liter in an hour? Fine. That's their top speed. Two liters. Now there's a queue. Three. The queue is out the door and down the street. The extra water has nowhere to go. It dilutes the salt, and your cells do what thirsty things do. They drink. Luckily, most cells can just swell a little. Unluckily, your brain lives in a box made of bone. Picture a sponge in a glass jar. The sponge grows. The jar doesn't. Headache, confusion, sleepiness: that's the sponge hitting the glass. And it's real. In 2007, a radio contest to drink the most water ended with a contestant dying that same day. After trainees ended up in hospital, the U.S. Army wrote a rule: no more than about a liter and a half an hour. So drink your water. Just give your kidneys time to finish the paperwork.
