# Review brief: plane-windows stills (video 12, "Why Are Airplane Windows Round?")

House look: flat 2D, white stickmen with a black outline (engineers in white coats and boiler suits, an investigator,
passengers, a person bending a paperclip), rich layered backgrounds, big titles and chips, "sentence" captions (white,
1-2 words). Serious mystery tone, no comedy. Quality bar: Blood Falls / Wind Cave (reference sheet
`videos/wind-cave/tests/sheet_2s.jpg`).

**NEW for this video, judge it too: the cinematic 2D upgrade.** Real perspective sets drawn from a moving 3D camera
(the 1954 test hangar and water tank, the airliner cabin aisle), depth of field (blurred foreground framing layers:
a passenger's shoulder, a seat back, a lamp shade, a pencil; soft backgrounds), varied camera angles (high crane over
the tank, medium-high over the gantry, over-the-shoulder at a cabin window, top-down on a map, low/side chase of the
plane, macro close-ups of a window corner and a paperclip, POV through a round window), a moving camera with few hard
cuts (s01 is one continuous crane from high over the tank down to a porthole; the porthole match-cuts into a modern
cabin window; s03 pushes through a window into the sky; the plane dissolves into a newspaper photo; s11 -> s11b is
one pan along one metal panel), per-scene light and colour grades with a soft vignette (cool grey-green 1950s hangar
with skylight shafts and water caustics, warm cabin, thin bright-blue altitude sky, sepia archive, teal under water,
dark navy diagram stage), light shafts, caustics, dust motes, rim light.

The Comet must look right: a slim 1950s jet airliner, four engines buried in the wing roots (oval intakes in the
leading edge, no pods), a rounded nose with a stepped cockpit, SQUARISH passenger windows (small corner radii), a white
top and a blue cheatline, no airline name or logo. The two 1954 losses are shown respectfully (a plane fading into
cloud, newspaper headlines, map markers): no explosions, fire, debris or people in danger.
