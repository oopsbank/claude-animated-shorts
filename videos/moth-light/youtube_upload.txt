TITLE
Why Do Moths Fly Into Lights? 🦋 #shorts

DESCRIPTION
In 2024, scientists released slow-motion films of moths around a lamp at night in a Costa Rican cloud forest. People have watched moths fly into flames and lights for thousands of years and never agreed why. The usual answers: they're drawn to the light, or they mistake it for the Moon. But on camera they didn't fly at the lamp; they flew around it. The clues: as they circled, they kept their backs turned toward the light; over a lamp pointing up, they flipped upside down and fell; and under a white sheet glowing above them like the sky, they flew straight through. For millions of years the brightest part of the night was the sky, so insects keep their backs toward the brightest light to know which way is up. Near a lamp, "up" points sideways, so they tilt, turn and end up circling it. Over a lamp, "up" points down, so they roll over and fall. Moths aren't attracted to the light. They're trapped by it.
