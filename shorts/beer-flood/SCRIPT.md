# The Street That Drowned in Beer

Status: DRAFT — waiting for approval.
Target: ~62 s at ~3.7 words/s. Voice: one steady deadpan tone, English.

## Facts (with sources)
- 17 October 1814, Meux & Co's Horse Shoe Brewery, near Tottenham Court Road, London.
- A wooden vat of porter about 22 ft high burst. It was held together by iron hoops. One hoop (~700 lb) had slipped. Storehouse clerk George Crick saw it and reported it in a note. He was told no harm would come of it.
- About an hour later the vat burst. It ruptured other vats and knocked down a brick wall. Estimates of the beer released run from ~610,000 L (the first vat) to over 1 million L (in total).
- The beer flooded St Giles, a crowded slum where families lived in cellars and basements. Eight people died (most accounts; 7–9 in others), all women and children.
- At the inquest the deaths were ruled accidental, an "Act of God". Nobody was held responsible.
- The brewery got back the excise duty it had paid on the lost beer (~£7,000–£7,250).
- Sources: https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Beer_Flood · https://www.history.com/articles/london-beer-flood · https://londonist.com/london/history/london-s-forgotten-disasters-the-great-beer-flood · https://www.damninteresting.com/beer-flood-claims-eight-souls/
- Avoid the myth that crowds drank beer from the streets. It is not well sourced.

## Script (English, ~228 words)
1. **Hook:** In 1814, a street in London was hit by a tidal wave. Of beer.
2. **Setting:** The Horse Shoe Brewery made porter, a dark beer, in wooden vats taller than a house. The biggest stood twenty-two feet high, held together by iron hoops.
3. **Action:** One afternoon, a clerk named George Crick noticed that one of those hoops had slipped. Seven hundred pounds of iron. He wrote a note. He was told it was fine.
4. **Snap:** An hour later, the vat burst.
5. **Escalation:** The wave smashed the vat beside it. Then the next one. A brick wall came down. Over a million litres of beer rolled into St Giles, one of the poorest streets in London, where whole families lived in cellars.
6. **Weight:** The cellars filled first. Eight people never got out.
7. **Explanation:** Here is why one hoop mattered. Liquid pushes outward, and the deeper it gets, the harder it pushes. Each hoop is like a belt after a huge dinner. Lose one notch, and the rest have to hold more. Then, pop.
8. **Deadpan double:** The court called it an Act of God, so nobody was to blame. Luckily for the brewery, the government even refunded the tax on the lost beer. Unluckily for the government, that was about seven thousand pounds.
9. **Callback twist:** And London learned a lesson that still holds today. When seven hundred pounds of iron falls off something, it is not fine.
