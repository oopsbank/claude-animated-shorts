# What If You Jumped Into a Hole Through the Earth?

## Facts (with sources)
- A straight fall through the Earth's diameter (vacuum tunnel) takes **38 min 11 s** with the real density profile (PREM);
  42 min 12 s for a uniform Earth. Any straight chord between two surface points takes between 38 and 42 min.
  A. R. Klotz, "The Gravity Tunnel in a Non-Uniform Earth", Am. J. Phys. 83, 231 (2015), arXiv:1308.1342.
  Round trip ≈ 76 min.
- Digging straight down from most of the continental US comes out in the Indian Ocean (SW of Australia), not China.
  (antipode maps; e.g. wtamu.edu Science Questions with Surprising Answers, 2012)
- Deepest hole ever drilled: Kola Superdeep Borehole, Russia, 12,262 m (1989); ~180 °C at the bottom, vs ~100 °C expected.
  Free fall from rest covers 12 km in ~49 s (constant g).
- Inner core ~5,200 °C, comparable to the Sun's surface (~5,500 °C).
- In free fall you feel weightless the whole way.
- Speed at the centre: sqrt(gR) ≈ 7.9 km/s for a uniform Earth, more for the real Earth: > ISS orbital speed (7.66 km/s),
  i.e. over 28,000 km/h.

## Script (English, ~200 words)
What if you jumped into a hole that went straight through the Earth?

First, you would not end up in China. Dig down from almost anywhere in the United States, and you come out in the
Indian Ocean.

So, a perfect tunnel. No air. Heatproof walls. You jump.

The deepest hole humans ever dug stopped at twelve kilometres. It was a hundred and eighty degrees at the bottom.
You pass it in under a minute.

Then the mantle. Then the core, about as hot as the surface of the Sun. And the whole way down, you feel weightless,
because you are falling exactly as fast as everything around you.

At the centre, you are going faster than the space station. Over twenty-eight thousand kilometres an hour.

Now gravity pulls you back. You slow down. And slow down. And thirty-eight minutes after you jumped, you pop out
on the other side, at exactly zero speed.

Here is the strange part. Dig a straight tunnel between any two cities, even next door, and the trip still takes
about forty minutes.

But if nobody grabs your hand at the top, you fall back in. Back and forth, every seventy-six minutes. Forever.
