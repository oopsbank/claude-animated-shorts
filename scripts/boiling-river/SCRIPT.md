# The River That Boils With No Volcano (working title)

Status: Producer draft v1, 2026-11-23. Topic picked by the Director (video 6). Same look and tone as videos 3-5: white stickmen with black outline, rich flat 2D, serious mystery, no comedy. Voice: Kokoro `am_adam`. Not voiced yet.

Opening rule from the YouTube Studio numbers (HANDOFF): the first 1-2 s show one striking image the viewer gets at once (steam over a jungle river, a small animal falling toward it), no number to decode.

## Sources
- Shanay-Timpishka ("boiled with the heat of the sun"), Huánuco region, Peru; ~6.4 km long; ~700 km from the nearest active volcano; Ruzo's measurements up to 99.1 °C; small animals that fall in die: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanay-timpishka ; https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/the-boiling-river-of-the-amazon ; https://scienceblog.com/sb-in-2011-peruvian-geologist-andr-s-ruzo-followed-his-grandfathers-story-into-the-amazon-and-confirmed-a-6-4-kilometre-stretch-of-river-that-runs-at-86-degrees-celsius-with-no-volcano-within-700-kilo/
- Ruzo heard the legend from his grandfather; colleagues said a boiling river was not possible; went in 2011: https://theworld.org/stories/2016/04/13/what-young-scientist-found-when-he-started-researching-famous-boiling-river-peru ; https://www.sciencefriday.com/articles/the-search-for-perus-boiling-river/
- Mechanism: non-volcanic hydrothermal system; water sinks deep, is heated by the rock, and rises through faults; starts as a cold stream and heats up where it crosses fault zones; geochemistry not magmatic: https://www.xylem.com/en-ie/resources/blog-posts/making-waves/the-amazons-legendary-boiling-river/ ; https://theworld.org/stories/2016/04/13/what-young-scientist-found-when-he-started-researching-famous-boiling-river-peru
- "Biggest known" = Ruzo's claim of the largest documented non-volcanic geothermal river.
- Simplified / dramatised: "cooked alive" (sources: small animals that fall in die, seen cooked); "for years scientists called it a legend" stands for Ruzo's colleagues saying it could not exist; "rain soaks in far away" is the leading hypothesis (water origin and age still being studied), stated as the accepted model.

## Voice-over (217 words)
1. Deep in the Amazon, there's a river so hot that animals that fall in are cooked alive.
2. For years, scientists called it a legend.
3. Then in 2011, geologist Andrés Ruzo went to see it, and dipped in a thermometer. Nearly one hundred degrees.
4. The obvious answer: a volcano underneath.
5. But the nearest active volcano is about seven hundred kilometres away.
6. Clue one. The river starts as a small, cold jungle stream. It only turns hot farther down.
7. Clue two. The heat pours in through cracks, right where the river crosses faults in the rock.
8. Clue three. Tests show the water carries no sign of a volcano.
9. So how does water get this hot, with no fire underneath?
10. The Earth itself is the stove. The deeper you go, the hotter the rock gets, volcano or not.
11. Rain soaks into the ground far away, and sinks kilometres deep, where the rock heats it.
12. Then pressure pushes it back up through those cracks, straight into the river.
13. Like a coffee machine. Cold water goes in, runs past something hot, and comes out steaming.
14. Here, the hot plate is the planet.
15. It's the biggest known hot river with no volcano behind it.
16. And every animal that falls in is a reminder that, deep under the jungle, the planet is still cooking.
