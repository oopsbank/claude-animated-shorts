# Brief: video 3, "The Shortcut" (new format: stickman life lesson)

Decided by the Director on 2026-11-22. Topic picked by the Producer (the Director had no preference among four).

## Format (new, replaces "Why/How + real phenomenon" for this video)
- **A life lesson told as a visual metaphor**, with a **voice-over** (the Director: "có giọng").
- Inspired by the *structure* of silent motivational stickman shorts (one metaphor, one message line), never their scripts, shots, titles or look. Study notes: `references/reports/` (to add), reference id JfDrx_ATgVE.
- **Cast: the stickman** (`stickman/`, `stickman/DRAWING_RULES.md`), taken off hold for this format. Expressive poses carry the acting: big, clear silhouettes, held 0.5-1.5 s, real in-betweens.
- **Look: 2D** (the Director: "video animation 2d"). Flat 2D backgrounds with a few depth layers, readable at phone size. Daylight or warm dusk by default; darkness only as a story beat.
- Keep: 62-66 s, "sentence" captions, ABSURD comedy (escalation, imagined cutaways framed as fantasy), a picture change every <= 1.5 s, a mid-action hook, an ending on a gag or a loop.

## Story seed (the Screenwriter owns the final story)
Two stickmen must reach the same place. One keeps taking shortcuts that each look smart and each throw him back further (escalating, absurd). The other walks the long road steadily and arrives first. Absurd final beat: the last shortcut leads straight into a dinosaur's mouth (or a better absurd payoff the panel prefers). Lesson: the shortcut is usually the long way round.

## Script rules for this format
- 225-240 words, plain spoken English, a narrator voice (one ElevenLabs pass, ~1,250 characters).
- Story engine adapted: line 1 shows someone doing something that looks wrong (the steady walker ignoring an obvious shortcut); the shortcut-taker mocks him in quoted dialogue and wins first; 3 escalating shortcut disasters with markers; the lesson late and short, with one everyday analogy; the shortcut-taker's way fails spectacularly; a punchline or loop line.
- Facts: keep real-world claims to zero or one; any claim needs a source URL and the fact checker still scores >= 9.
- Original title, checked against `references/titles.md`.
