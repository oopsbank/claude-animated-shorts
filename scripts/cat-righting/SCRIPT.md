# Why Do Cats Always Land on Their Feet? (video 8, `cat-righting`, approved 2026-11-24)

"Why" format on the **Blood Falls formula** (HANDOFF): a human witness + year in line 1 (Étienne-Jules Marey, Paris, 1894), ONE hero image (the cat upside down in mid-air, with the 1894 film strip as the colour/texture motif), the wrong answer stated then killed in the next line, 3 one-fact clues + a riddle, the reveal in short sentences, one everyday analogy (a spinning office chair), the flip ("The cat didn't break physics. It used it."), loop back to Paris 1894. Serious, curious tone, no comedy. Voice Kokoro `am_adam` 1.16: `audio/cat-righting/` (1958 frames = 65.27 s).

- Simplified: "nothing falling can start to spin on its own" = conservation of angular momentum for a rigid body; the office chair is an approximate analogy.

## Voice-over (224 words)
1. In 1894, a scientist in Paris dropped a cat and filmed it.
2. Upside down, with nothing to push against, the cat flipped over and landed on its feet.
3. And that seemed to break a law of physics.
4. Nothing falling can start to spin on its own.
5. The obvious answer: the cat pushed off the scientist's hands.
6. But the photos showed it dropping straight, with no spin. The turn began in mid-air.
7. Clue one. Cats born without a tail do it too.
8. Clue two. In every frame, the cat is bent in the middle.
9. Clue three. In 1968, NASA-funded scientists put a gymnast in a spacesuit to copy it.
10. So how do you turn over with nothing to push on?
11. The cat isn't one solid object. It's two halves, joined by a bendy spine.
12. First, it tucks its front legs in and stretches its back legs out.
13. Tucked, the front half turns easily. Stretched, the back half barely moves.
14. Then it swaps. Back legs in, front legs out. Now the back half catches up.
15. The twists cancel out, so no law is broken. But the cat ends up flipped.
16. Like turning a spinning chair just by swinging your arms and legs in turns.
17. The cat didn't break physics. It used it.
18. And every cat still lands the way that cat did in Paris, in 1894.
