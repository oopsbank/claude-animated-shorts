# Why Do Zebras Have Stripes? (video 9, `zebra-stripes`, approved 2026-11-24)

"Why" format on the **Blood Falls formula** (HANDOFF): a human witness + year in line 1 (the 2016 experiment in England), ONE strange hero image (a brown horse wearing a zebra-striped coat in a green English field, horse flies circling), TWO wrong answers stated and killed at once (camouflage, cooling), 3 one-fact clues + a riddle, the mechanism, one everyday analogy (striped stairs), the flip ("The stripes aren't hiding the zebra. They're hiding the landing spot."), loop back to the 2016 field. Serious, curious tone, no comedy. Voice Kokoro `am_adam` 1.16: `audio/zebra-stripes/` (1951 frames = 65.03 s).

## Sources
- Caro et al. 2019, "Benefits of zebra stripes: Behaviour of tabanid flies around zebras and horses", PLOS ONE 14(2): e0210831. Horses at a livery in North Somerset, UK, summers 2016-2017; flies circled zebras and horses alike but landed less on zebras because they failed to slow down; horses in striped coats got fewer landings on the covered parts, none fewer on their bare heads. https://bristol.ac.uk/news/2019/february/zebra-stripes.html ; https://www.ucdavis.edu/news/how-zebra-stripes-disrupt-flies-flight-patterns
- Melin et al. 2016, PLOS ONE: beyond ~50 m in daylight lions/hyenas can hardly resolve the stripes; on open grassland zebras are as easy to spot as other similar-sized animals, so not camouflage. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4723339
- Horváth et al. 2018, Scientific Reports: water barrels covered with zebra hide / striped coats were not cooler than grey ones. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6008466
- Caro et al. 2014, Nature Communications, "The function of zebra stripes": striping matches areas of biting-fly activity; no consistent support for camouflage, heat or social hypotheses. https://www.nature.com/articles/ncomms4535
- Kojima et al. 2019, PLOS ONE: Japanese Black cows painted with black-and-white stripes had significantly fewer biting flies. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6776349/
- Simplified: "the stripes scramble its sense of where the surface is" is the leading idea (the exact visual mechanism is still debated); "deadly diseases" = tabanid-borne diseases such as trypanosomiasis and equine infections; zebra hair is short (Caro).

## Voice-over (210 words)
1. In 2016, scientists in England dressed horses in zebra-striped coats. Then they watched the flies.
2. Because nobody could agree why zebras have stripes at all.
3. The obvious answer: camouflage, to hide from lions.
4. But on open grassland, lions spot zebras easily. Past fifty metres, they can't even see the stripes.
5. Maybe stripes keep them cool? But barrels wrapped in zebra skin got just as hot.
6. Clue one. Zebras have the boldest stripes where biting flies are worst.
7. Clue two. Horse flies spread deadly diseases, and zebra hair is too short to stop a bite.
8. Clue three. On the horses in costume, fewer flies landed, except on their bare heads.
9. So what do stripes do to a fly?
10. A horse fly comes in fast, then slows down at the last moment to land.
11. On a plain horse, it brakes and touches down.
12. On stripes, it doesn't slow down. It bumps into the coat or zooms past.
13. The stripes scramble its sense of where the surface is.
14. Farmers in Japan painted stripes on cows. Far fewer bites.
15. Like stairs painted with stripes: you can't tell where to put your foot.
16. The stripes aren't hiding the zebra. They're hiding the landing spot.
17. And on those striped horses in 2016, the flies kept missing.
