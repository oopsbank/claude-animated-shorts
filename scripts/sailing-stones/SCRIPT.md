Fiction, made up for the story: the "alien rock tours" guide, his tourists, his prices, his van and every line he says; the cousins' looks and the camp. The two cousins and the geologists are drawn as our own generic bean-heads, never as the real people, and none of them are named. No brands.

**Analogy check:** "a giant window sliding across a wet kitchen floor and shoving the fridge": the pane is big and thin (it catches the wind like a sail), the wet mud is slippery, the rock is heavy. Physically fair.

## Voice-over (one line = one shot in `shots.json`)

1. Two cousins hid GPS trackers inside fifteen rocks, then waited for the rocks to run away.
2. You see, on a dry lake in Death Valley,
3. some rocks heavier than a man leave long trails,
4. and nobody had ever seen one move.
5. A local guide sells tours to see the culprits.
6. "Rocks don't walk, pal," he laughs.
7. "Aliens drag them at night.
8. Twenty bucks a ticket."
9. And his tours sell out.
10. First, in 1948, geologists blamed whirlwinds,
11. or maybe floods. Nobody could prove either one.
12. Then, in the late sixties, a geologist named thirty rocks
13. and fenced some in with stakes. One escaped anyway,
14. and in seven years his team never saw one move.
15. The guide doubles his prices.
16. So in 2011, the cousins set up trackers, cameras
17. and a weather station, ready to wait ten years.
18. "See you in ten years, nerds!"
19. Two years later, they're standing on the lake bed
20. when dozens of rocks start sliding, in broad daylight.
21. Winter rain had made a shallow pond,
22. and overnight it froze into ice millimetres thick.
23. The late-morning sun cracked it into huge floating panes.
24. A light breeze pushed the panes,
25. and the panes pushed the rocks,
26. a few metres a minute.
27. It's like a giant window sliding across a wet kitchen floor
28. and shoving the fridge.
29. And the guide? Snoring in his van.
30. His alien tours only run at night.
