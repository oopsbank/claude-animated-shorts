It's 3 a.m. Emma is asleep upstairs. Then, downstairs, something clicks.
Footsteps. A soft ticking. Then silence.
She creeps downstairs. The room is empty. The front door is locked.
Just the old house settling, she tells herself.
But the next night, it happens again. And the next.
Clue one. Nothing is ever stolen.
Clue two. The lock is never broken. Whoever comes in has a key.
Clue three. The old clock in the hall came with the house. She never winds it. It never stops.
So who walks into a stranger's house every night, just to touch a clock?
Emma hides a camera on the bookshelf, and waits.
3 a.m. The door opens. An old man steps inside, in his slippers.
He walks straight to the clock and winds it. Slowly. Carefully.
Then he pats the wood, like saying goodnight, and leaves.
The next morning, Emma knocks on the house next door.
He lived in her house for forty years. The clock was his late wife's. He promised her it would never stop.
He sold the house. He kept the key. And he kept his promise.
It was never a burglar. It was a man keeping his word.
Now, at 3 a.m., when Emma hears a click downstairs, she just smiles, and goes back to sleep.
