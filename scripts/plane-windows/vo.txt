So why would a corner tear first?
Up high, the cabin is pumped full of air so you can breathe. The body swells slightly, like a balloon.
Every landing, it shrinks back. One flight, one squeeze.
Around a round window, the stretch flows smoothly past. At a sharp corner, it piles into one tiny point.
Squeeze after squeeze, a tiny crack grows there, until the metal gives way. That's metal fatigue.
It's like bending a paperclip: once is fine. Do it a hundred times, and it snaps.
Round windows aren't about looks. They're about not tearing open.
