# This Cave Has Been Breathing Since 1881 (working title)

Status: Producer draft v1, 2026-11-23. Topic picked by the Director (video 7). Built on **HANDOFF › The Blood Falls formula** (video 3 went viral): one impossible image named by a body metaphor ("the hole was breathing"), a witness + year in line 1, the wrong answer killed in the next line, 3 one-fact clues, a riddle, the metaphor flipped in two short sentences ("The cave isn't breathing. The sky is."), one everyday analogy, a loop to 1881. The Director asks for **meticulous, beautiful graphics** (Blood Falls level). Voice: Kokoro `am_adam`. Not voiced yet.

## Sources
- NPS, Wind Cave history: 1881, brothers Jesse and Tom Bingham heard a whistling; wind from the small natural entrance blew Tom's hat off; Jesse later returned and the wind had reversed and sucked his hat in (the NPS tells it "as the story goes"); natural entrance ~8 x 10 in: https://www.nps.gov/wica/learn/historyculture/cave-exploration.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/wica/historyculture/birth-of-a-national-park-a-national-park-is-created.htm
- NPS, Wind Cave geology: ~167 miles (~269 km) of known cave as of 2024 under ~1.25 sq mi; a barometric "breathing" cave — air moves in or out to equalise the pressure of cave and outside air: https://home.nps.gov/wica/learn/nature/wind-cave-geology.htm ; https://home.nps.gov/wica/learn/news/exploration-pushes-wind-cave-to-5th-longest-in-the-world.htm
- Storm = falling pressure → cave air rushes out (Wikipedia's explanation of the 1881 outflow): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_Cave_National_Park ; NPS brochure (barometric wind studies suggest only a small part of the cave is known): https://www.npshistory.com/publications/wica/brochures/2014.pdf
- Simplified / dramatised: the hat story is the park's traditional account (which brother lost which hat varies); "smaller than a sheet of paper" uses the NPS 8 x 10 in size (other sources give 10 x 14 in); "blows out before a storm / pulls in in fair weather" simplifies "flows from high to low pressure"; "a tunnel with another opening" stands for the natural first guess, not a documented claim. The cave is sacred to the Lakota (emergence site); the video does not tell that story and does not say the brothers "discovered" it.

## Voice-over (219 words)
1. In 1881, two brothers in South Dakota heard a whistle coming from the ground.
2. They found a small hole in the rock. And the hole was breathing.
3. Wind rushed out so hard it blew Tom Bingham's hat off.
4. The obvious answer: a tunnel, with another opening somewhere.
5. But no other entrance was ever found. Just one hole, smaller than a sheet of paper.
6. Clue one. Later, the hole breathed in. It sucked Jesse's hat inside.
7. Clue two. It follows the weather. Before a storm, it blows out. In fair weather, it pulls in.
8. Clue three. Behind the hole is a maze: over two hundred and sixty kilometres of tunnels, still not fully explored.
9. So what makes a hole in the ground breathe?
10. The air above us is heavy. It presses on everything.
11. When a storm comes, that pressure drops, so the cave's air pushes harder and rushes out.
12. When the storm passes, the pressure rises, and the sky pushes air back in.
13. With that many tunnels, a tiny change moves a huge amount of air through one small hole.
14. Like a bag of chips on a plane, puffing up as the air outside thins.
15. The cave isn't breathing. The sky is.
16. And whenever a storm rolls over those hills, the little hole still breathes out, just like in 1881.
