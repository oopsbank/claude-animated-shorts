Two cousins hid GPS trackers inside fifteen rocks, then waited for the rocks to run away. They even named one Gary. On a Death Valley lake bed, some rocks as heavy as a fridge leave long trails. You see, nobody had ever filmed one moving. A guide in alien antennae laughs at them. "Scientists blamed wind, then ice. A fenced-in rock escaped anyway! It's aliens, pal. Twenty bucks." His night-only tours sell out. Month one: a dust storm buries their camera, and Gary. "Leaf-blowing aliens," says the guide. "Thirty bucks." Year one: a flash flood carries off their tent, cousins included. They paddle home on a cooler. Gary steers. "Alien rock-racing league!" he yells. "Forty bucks." Year two, last try: he livestreams their failure to his fans. Rain floods the lake bed; cold nights freeze it, sleeping cousins included. Even his imaginary aliens doze off. One rare sunny morning, the ice crackles, and dozens of rocks start sliding. Gary too, tracker logging. "Alien air-hockey final!" cries the guide. "The referee's a rock! Fifty bucks!" The sun breaks the phone-thin ice into huge panes; a light breeze shoves them into rocks. Same game. No aliens. His livestream shows rocks sliding in broad daylight.
